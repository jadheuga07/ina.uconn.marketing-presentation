Why we need to be more outstanding than students were before. One: in 2022 about one salaried opening in 15 accepted candidates with no experience, in 2026 it is one in 50. Two: applicants per opening have more than tripled, seasoned and entry-level candidates alike, so students face adults with a track record and better connections. Three: recent graduates are now unemployed at 5.6 percent against 4.2 percent for all workers, the reverse of 2010. A degree alone no longer separates a student, real experience does. That is what INA gives members.

02 · Outreach program

The INA Outreach Program

Four steps from a peer match to a student-created opportunity.

1234
The program in four steps: match, outreach, project and final delivery. The next slides show each step.

Step 1: Present, Match, Build

Our peer-matching workshops run in three stages, and students leave with a team.

Students present themselves or an idea, at a workshop or on the website, are matched on interests and skills, and leave with a team.

Step 2: INA opens the doors

INA is the mediator: our name and network introduce each project until someone says yes.

INA uses its name and network to introduce each project to companies, mentors, institutions, alumni, professors and professionals until someone says yes. This is where institutions come in.

Step 3: the weekly rhythm

Once a mentor accepts to follow the team, this cycle repeats every week.

Once a mentor accepts, the team works, writes a weekly report, presents it to INA members and publishes it on the website. Every week.

Step 4: the opportunity delivered

The team presents the finished project, writes the final report, and it goes on the website.

The team presents the finished project to a board of the mentor, INA and a professional, writes the final report, and INA verifies and publishes it.

03 · A worked example

Example: an equity research analysis/pitch

Students who do not know each other want to build an equity research analysis/pitch and have it reviewed by a professional.

1

Match

They meet at a peer-matching workshop, pick one company and split the work: industry research, financial model, valuation, slides.

2

INA makes the connection

INA reaches out and connects them with an analyst or a UConn finance professor.

3

Mentored research

Each week they post a report: industry and competitors, then a financial model (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow), then the valuation. The mentor reviews each one.

4

The pitch

They present a buy, hold or sell call with a 12-month price target, then publish the final report, verified by INA.

03 · Senior fall

The interview. This time, he has a story.

Interviewer

Your resume has no internship. Tell me about something you built.

An equity research pitch, built with three students I met through INA. I owned the valuation: a full financial model and two methods. Our mentor, a portfolio manager, pushed back on our growth assumptions, so we rebuilt the model and cut our price target. The final report is published and verified by INA.

Interviewer

A student project is not a job. Why should that count as experience?

Because it was judged like one. Eight weekly reports, a real professional reviewing every number, and deadlines we did not set ourselves. I can defend each figure in that model right now, and I learned to change my mind when the data says so. That is the job of an analyst.

Offer letter

Congratulations, Brandon.

We are pleased to offer you a summer analyst position.

Read it as a short dialogue. The interviewer challenges Brandon twice, and he answers both times with specifics. The point: INA work becomes evidence a recruiter can test.
