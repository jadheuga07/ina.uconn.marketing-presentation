Openings and applicants: Forbes, August 2, 2026, reporting Cadient's analysis of 7,984 salaried openings from a stable group of employers, 2022 to 2026. Why students need to stand out more than before. One: in 2022 about one salaried opening in 15 accepted someone with no experience; in 2026 it is one in 50. Two: there are more than three times as many applicants for every opening, so students compete with adults who already have a track record. Three: recent graduates are unemployed at 5.6 percent against 4.1 percent for all workers, the reverse of 2010. A degree alone no longer separates a student. Real experience does.

The INA Outreach Program

The structured service INA offers: it takes students from an idea to a finished, mentored project with an INA certificate.

What it is

One program, four steps, the same for every student and every idea.

1Match2Outreach3Project4Final

Run by INA from start to finish: we organize the match, make the introductions, keep the weekly rhythm and award the certificate.

What it represents

Initiative

Students create their own opportunities instead of waiting for one.

Connection

Proof

Experience a recruiter can check, backed by an INA certificate.

This is the service INA actually offers: a structured program, the same four steps for every student and every idea, run by INA from start to finish. Then what it represents, one tap each: initiative, connection, and proof. Next, the four steps one by one, following Brandon.

Step 1: Present, Match, Build

Our peer-matching workshops run in three stages, and students leave with a team.

Brandon, step 1

At a workshop, he meets three students who want to build a stock pitch.

No team A team of four

Students present themselves or an idea, at a workshop or on the website, are matched on interests and skills, and leave with a team. Last tap: Brandon meets three students who want to build a stock pitch. No team becomes a team of four.

Step 2: INA opens the doors

INA is the mediator: our name and network introduce each project until someone says yes.

Brandon, step 2

INA introduces their project until a portfolio manager says yes.

No network A mentor

INA uses its name and network to introduce each project to companies, mentors, institutions, alumni, professors and professionals until someone says yes. This is where professionals, companies and institutions come in. Last tap: for Brandon, a portfolio manager says yes. No network becomes a mentor.

Step 3: the weekly rhythm

Once a mentor accepts to follow the team, this cycle repeats every week.

Brandon, step 3

Eight weekly reports, each one reviewed by his mentor.

Abandoned alone Finished together

Once a mentor accepts, the team works, writes a weekly report, presents it to INA members and publishes it on the website. Every week. Last tap: eight weekly reports, each reviewed by his mentor. Abandoned alone becomes finished together.

Step 4: the opportunity delivered

The team presents the finished project, writes the final report, and it goes on the website.

Brandon, step 4

His team presents the pitch and earns an INA certificate.

No experience A real project

The team presents the finished project to a board of the mentor, INA and a professional, writes the final report, and earns an INA certificate. Last tap: his team presents the pitch and earns an INA certificate. No experience becomes a real project.

02 · Senior fall

The interview. This time, he has a story.

The visa checkbox is still there. But now his application has something nobody else has.

Interviewer

Your resume has no internship. Tell me about something you built.

An equity research pitch, built with three students I met through INA. I owned the valuation: a full financial model and two methods. Our mentor, a portfolio manager, pushed back on our growth assumptions, so we rebuilt the model and cut our price target. The final report carries an INA certificate.

Interviewer

A student project is not a job. Why should that count as experience?

Because it was judged like one. Eight weekly reports, a real professional reviewing every number, and deadlines we did not set ourselves. I can defend each figure in that model right now, and I learned to change my mind when the data says so. That is the job of an analyst.

Offer letter

Congratulations, Brandon.

We are pleased to offer you a summer analyst position.

Read it as a short dialogue. The interviewer challenges Brandon twice, and he answers both times with specifics. The point: INA work becomes evidence a recruiter can test.
